How it works

Top. The highest daily close since the last halving. It counts as confirmed after a 20% fall.

Clock 1. Days from top to bottom in past cycles. The bar spans the shortest to the longest. The marker is the median.

Clock 2. Days from bottom to the next halving in past cycles, counted back from the next halving date. That date is an estimate from the block height.

Clock 3. Days from a halving to the cycle top in past cycles. It shows only until a top is confirmed.

The clocks use the same few cycles. They are not independent proof.

Bottom looks in. The low is at least 60% of the shortest past bear after the top. It has held 90 days. Price is 20% above it. This is a rule of thumb.

Faber. The 10-month average uses the last 9 closed months plus today's price. That matches a standard TradingView monthly 10-SMA. The rule is read at month end.

Realized price. Realized cap divided by supply. Providers differ by a few percent. The caution zone is the floor up to 1.25 times the floor.

Next cycle. Halving dates come from the live block height. When a halving happens, a new cycle starts. The finished cycle's top and bottom are found from price history and join the averages. Nothing needs editing.

Descriptive tool, not financial advice. A handful of past cycles is a very small sample. The clocks assume this cycle rhymes with earlier ones. It may not.
